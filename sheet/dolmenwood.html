Read this first

Constitution 5 is the defining fact of this character. You lose 2 hit points per level for the whole of your career, and if the d8 comes up 1, 2 or 3 you start play on a single hit point. A goblin with a rock can end you. It also costs you 20% of all XP earned, because Constitution is one of the hunter’s two prime abilities.

Play accordingly. Stay at bowshot. Let the animal companion scout. Do not be the one who opens the door.

Kit

Click any line to rewrite it, use the cross to drop something, and add new gear at the bottom. Arrows and coin are kept in the play tracker, so those two lines follow it and cannot be edited here.

The clothes, backpack and belt pouch are what every character starts with. The armour, shield, bow and dagger are a legal hunter equipment roll. The bow needs both hands, so the shield is only on your arm when the bow is stowed and the dagger is out. Decide which one you are holding at the start of each fight rather than mid-round. Custom-fitted armour for a grimalkin takes 2d6 days to make or alter.

Chisel, sledgehammer, shovel, ink and quill. That is a barrow-digging kit with a notebook attached, in a wood named after its standing stones. Play it as deliberate. Watch it against your encumbrance too, because a hammer and a shovel together are a great deal of iron for a three-foot cat, and speed is most of what keeps you alive.

The whistle is audible to every living thing except dogs. Nobody has yet found the use for it. Somebody will.

What a grimalkin is

A fairy cat wearing a small humanoid shape. You stand about three feet tall, you are immortal in the sense that nothing natural will kill you, and no ordinary disease can touch you. You are also small enough that most of the woods is Large, which is why your armour class improves against big things.

Chester form

Turn into a fat house cat

No limit on how often. In this shape you have AC 12 and attacks that do 1 point of damage each. You cannot use weapons, glamours, or hunter skills while you are a cat, and you can only shift back when nothing sentient is watching. It is a scouting and hiding tool, not a combat one.

Wilder form

Once a day, when you are nearly dead

Available only when you drop below half hit points. You heal 2d6 immediately, get AC 13, a +2 attack bonus and 1d4 damage, and it lasts 2d4 rounds. The catch is that you cannot tell friend from enemy. Warn the party before you use it.

On 2 hit points you cannot reach the trigger. Half of 2 is 1, so “below half” means 0, and 0 is dead. This ability switches on at level 2 and stays off until then. Ask your referee whether they will read the trigger as 1 hit point or fewer.

Glamour

Fool’s Gold

Touch copper coins and they look like gold to mortals. Range is whatever you touch. It lasts 1d6 minutes. Any mortal who looks at the coins may Save Versus Spell to see the trick for what it is, and other fairies are not fooled at all.

You may glamour 20 coins per day per level, so 20 a day right now. Rolled 10 on the fairy magic table.

Like every glamour it needs no words or gestures, cannot be disrupted, and costs your action for the round.

Worth saying plainly: this does nothing in a fight. It is a swindler’s tool with a 1d6 minute fuse, which means the plan has to include being somewhere else when it lapses. For a cat with 7 gold and a shovel, that is probably the right kind of problem.

Odds and ends

Cold iron, and giant rats

Cold iron weapons do 1 extra damage to you, which on your hit point total is a genuine threat rather than a footnote. On the other side of the ledger: eat a giant rodent over the course of a turn and you get a hit point back. It is not dignified. Take it anyway.

What a hunter does

Combat aptitude: martial

You fight. You do not cast.

The book files the hunter under martial, the same bracket as the fighter and the knight. That is why you start on a +1 attack bonus while a thief or a magician starts on +0, and why you may carry any weapon that is not Large. Nothing in your class advancement is spellcasting. The one piece of magic you own is the glamour, and that comes from your kindred, not your class.

Level 2 costs 2,250 XP. At your −20% that means 2,813 awarded before it lands, and it is the level where wilder form finally switches on.

Animal companion

Bond with a beast

Make a Charisma check to win an animal over: roll d6, add your Charisma modifier, and you need 4 or more. Your modifier is 0, so it is a flat coin toss. One companion at a time, and it is loyal. With your hit points this is the closest thing you have to a front line, so choose something that can take a hit.

Missile bonus

+1 to hit with anything you throw or shoot

Stacked with your class attack bonus that is +2 with a bow. The hunter’s equipment entry settles the question of longbows for you: Small characters take a shortbow in place of one, so a shortbow it is. 1d6 damage at 50, 100 and 150 feet.

Trophies

Take something from what you kill

Harvested trophies grant combat bonuses later. Start keeping a list on the back of this sheet from your first session.

Four skills

The reason the party brings you

Alertness and Stalking start at 6, and Tracking and Survival at 5. Survival is a skill every character has; the hunter is the one who gets better at it. These are the rolls that turn a trackless wood into somewhere you can navigate, so make them constantly rather than saving them for emergencies.

Appearance and bearing

Violet fur, a snub nose, and ear tufts nobody would call modest. The black wools do nothing for your armour class and a great deal for your outline after dark. Ask your referee whether they are worth something on a Stalking roll at night; the rules do not say, and most referees will give you it.

Fastidious is the word people use. You groom before a fight and again after it, and you will not sleep in a wet cloak. Keep playing that. A habit the referee can reach for is worth more at the table than a line on the sheet.
